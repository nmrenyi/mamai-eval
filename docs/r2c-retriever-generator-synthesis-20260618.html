R2c Synthesis — Where to Invest in Retrieval & Generation

2026-06-18 · config-v0.2.0 · MAM-AI on-device medical assistant (Kenyan nurses/midwives). Synthesises two studies: the reranker line (reports/r2c-rerank/r2c-reranker-results-20260613.html) and the embedder bake-off (docs/r2c-embedder-report-20260616.html), plus the unified generator×retriever matrix run 2026-06-18.

Bottom line. Two independent retrieval upgrades — a fine-tuned reranker and a better embedder (EmbeddingGemma) — both won decisively offline and failed to move answers online. A controlled generator A/B then showed the missing lever: swapping Gemma 4 → Gemma 3n roughly doubles answer quality, uniformly, on every retriever. The generator is the binding constraint; retrieval is not. Recommended order of investment: (1) generator (3n + RAG-grounding) ≫ (2) adopt EmbeddingGemma (cheap, low-risk, but ~0 online gain today) ≫ (3) corpus.

2. The core finding — offline gains don't reach the user

Both retrieval levers cleared their offline bars by a wide margin, yet neither moved end-to-end answer quality. The reranker line ruled out "the corpus is the problem" too: a coverage audit found the corpus holds a relevant chunk for ~89% (strict) / ~96% (lenient) of kenya queries — only ~10.6% truly absent. So the chain retrieval → corpus → answer is intact up to the last step, and breaks there.

If better retrieval over a sufficient corpus still doesn't lift answers — and on kenya, injected context actively hurts Gemma 4 (no-RAG 0.178 > every RAG arm) — the bottleneck is downstream. The generator can't convert retrieved context into better answers.

4. Recommendations — ranked by leverage

#ActionExpected end-to-end gainEffort / risk
1 Generator: move back to Gemma 3n E4B (or otherwise recover its completeness) LARGE kenya ~2×; hb +8 pp completeness Med / gated — resolve two open questions first (below)
2 G-RAG: ground the generator in retrieved context (prompt/format + RAG-aware fine-tune) so context helps instead of hurting HIGH potential — the only path that turns retrieval into answers High / research workstream
3 Retriever: adopt EmbeddingGemma-300M (replace Gecko) ~0 today best deployable retriever; only retriever that ever beats no-RAG (3n/kenya) Low / low — deployable, faster embed; do it, but don't expect answer movement until #1–2 land
4 Do NOT ship the reranker; do NOT fine-tune the embedder (R2d) none reranker doesn't transfer to kenya; retrieval already strong — / avoids wasted effort
5 Corpus expansion (the ~10% strictly-absent kenya queries) minor bounded by ~10.6% absent Med / below G-RAG in priority
Two gates before reversing the device's generator choice. (a) Safety is metric-dependent: 3n is more complete and the healthbench rubric penalty slightly favours it (0.363 vs 0.373), but the earlier kenya-SAQ harm-flag metric had 3n worse (~0.32 vs ~0.19). Resolve "is 3n safe enough?" on the harm axis first. (b) Deployability / why 4 was chosen: 3n was the predecessor on-device, so it should run — but confirm what the 3n→4 switch was bought for (size? latency? vendor default?) so the reversal is deliberate, not a regression trade.

5. Caveats

  • End-to-end sets are small/medium (kenya 312, hb 1209); the pre-registered noise floor is ~5 pp (1/√n). The marginal retriever cells sit within it; the generator effect is far above it.
  • Cluster generators are GGUF Q4_0 proxies for the on-device .litertlm; the direction (3n ≫ 4) is robust, exact magnitudes will shift on-device.
  • Deployability for EmbeddingGemma was measured on a flagship (SM8750) as a proxy; confirm latency on a true low-/mid Zanzibar device before release (CPU+int8 only — GPU delegate is unusable).
  • The single positive retriever cell (EmbeddingGemma×3n kenya) is one cell; treat it as "doesn't hurt and may help once the generator improves," not as a standalone win.
