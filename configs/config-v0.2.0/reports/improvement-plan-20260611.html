MAM-AI v0.2 — Improvement Plan

2026-06-11 · config-v0.2.0 · synthesizes the retrieval audit (mamaretrieval), generator-faithfulness eval, and Phase B end-to-end results (MCQ / SAQ / HealthBench rubric). Nothing here has been implemented yet — this document records what we should do and why, for the next improvement cycle.

TL;DR — where the system stands and what to do

Diagnosis (consistent across all reports): the system is safe but unhelpful, and RAG currently adds noise instead of value.

  • Zero "dangerous" safety ratings anywhere; active commission of wrong/harmful content ≈ 2%. Safety is not the bottleneck.
  • End-to-end usefulness is low: SAQ key-fact recall 0.178 (kenya), HealthBench weighted_met ≈ −0.016 (oss_eval) — driven by deflection (refusing scope or deferring to "see a doctor" without first-line content), not by knowledge gaps.
  • The deployed retriever (Gecko, top-3, threshold 0.0) delivers a bundle with no useful chunk in ~19% of queries and no top-tier chunk in ~56% — and we inject it anyway. RAG measurably hurts (MCQ −1.8 pp; SAQ scope-refusals rise with RAG).
  • When the generator does engage with good context, it is largely faithful (calibrated true-hallucination ≈ 9%, band 3–17%) — and a notable share of contradiction flags trace to the guideline corpus contradicting itself, a problem that grows as retrieval improves.

Priorities: P0 stop injecting noise (retrieval threshold) and fix deflection (system prompt) — both cheap, both attack proven harms. P1 upgrade the on-device retriever (embedder bake-off / hybrid / rerank). P2 query rewriting and corpus cleanup.

1. Retrieval improvements

Evidence base: mamaretrieval v2 audit — 3,185 queries, 6 retrievers, 230,964 LLM-graded (query, chunk) pairs (Qwen3.5-397B judge, graded rubric 0–6, validated 95%/85% vs Opus 4.7 at the lenient/strict thresholds). Deployed configuration: Gecko_1024_quant.tflite, k=3, similarity_threshold: 0.0.

Three distinct problems, each with a different fix:

  1. Ranking problem (lenient): the useful chunk usually is in Gecko's top-20 (HR@20 = 0.977) but not its top-3 (0.814). Fixable by reranking / depth / fusion.
  2. Structural embedding problem (strict): Gecko never surfaces ~65% of complete/actionable chunks at any rank in its top-20 (strict pool recall flat at 0.345, vs voyage 0.768; barely above BM25's 0.283). Not fixable by ranking — only by a better embedder. Critically, this deficit is concentrated exactly on the dose/threshold/management-step chunks the generator needs.
  3. Noise-injection problem: with threshold 0.0 we always inject 3 chunks — including the ~19% of queries where none are useful. The Phase B reports show this is not free: it costs accuracy and triggers refusals.
Headroom is proven: voyage/octen show the corpus supports ~0.99 lenient / ~0.75 strict HR@3. The gap is in our retriever, not the corpus. Known ceiling: ~25% of queries have no strict-relevant chunk in any retriever's top-3 — targets should be set against that ceiling, not 100%.
Scope caveat: all numbers above use clean English queries generated from corpus chunks — a favorable distribution. Retrieval quality on the deployment distribution (kenya/whb vignettes, messy phrasing, multi-turn) is unmeasured.
Cheap fix: judge the bundles the app actually injected — Gecko's top-3 is already saved in the +RAG result JSONs. ~1.1k pairs for the SAQ sets, <1 h of judging → HR@3 / P@3 on the real distribution, enough for the R1 threshold decision. (It can't tell a retriever miss from a corpus gap; that attribution needs the full 6-retriever union pool, ~25k pairs — only worth it if the question matters.)

R1 — Relevance threshold tuning P0

Goal: never inject a chunk we don't believe is relevant; fall back to no-RAG when the bundle is junk. At this stage we prefer abstention over noise.

  • Why now: this is the only place retrieval actively harms the system today. MCQ −1.8 pp with RAG; SAQ zero-recall rows shift toward scope-refusal under RAG (50% → 67%), e.g. geographic over-refusal when Zanzibar-specific context is injected into a Kenya vignette.
  • How: calibrate a cosine-similarity cutoff offline against the existing 230k graded labels (sweep thresholds over Gecko's top-3 scores; trade injected-chunk precision vs abstention rate vs lost hits). Filter per-chunk, not all-or-nothing — keep 1–2 good chunks rather than forcing 3-or-none.
  • Deployment: a config change (params.json retrieval.similarity_threshold) already plumbed through the app — near-zero engineering. Abstaining also frees context/prefill budget on-device.
  • Known difficulty — Gecko's scores are poorly separated: across the 3,185 audit queries, the mean cosine gap between rank 1 and rank 20 is only 0.09, and the rank-1 score distribution (p5–p95: 0.685–0.853) overlaps heavily with rank-20's (0.573–0.772). A single global cutoff may therefore have weak discriminating power. If the offline sweep confirms this, fall back to relative rules — e.g. keep only chunks within a margin δ of the query's top-1 score, or inject fewer chunks when the score curve is flat — rather than an absolute threshold.
  • Caveats: validate the cutoff per-dataset (score calibration may drift across query types); re-calibrate whenever the embedder changes (ties to R2). Gate the final value on an end-to-end SAQ ±RAG A/B, not retrieval metrics alone.

R2 — On-device retriever upgrade P1

Goal: close as much of the Gecko-vs-top-tier gap as possible under strict on-device constraints (CPU/NPU, quantized, LiteRT-compatible, latency and memory budgets). Voyage/octen are reference points, not options.

Four candidate approaches, in recommended exploration order — the first three can be simulated offline for free from the existing audit artifacts (rankings + 230k labels) before any device work:

a. Hybrid retrieval (Gecko + BM25, RRF fusion) Run BM25 alongside Gecko on-device (lexically complementary: drug names, doses, exact terms), fuse with reciprocal-rank fusion. Implementation is nearly free: the app already uses SQLite, and SQLite FTS5 ships BM25 ranking natively — an index table plus one query. Caveat: lexical match is monolingual; it will not bridge Swahili/German queries to English chunks. Latency realism: a cross-encoder scores 20 (query, chunk) pairs per turn — only a very small model fits the budget; using the resident Gemma as reranker means ~20 short prefills and is likely too slow. Reranking cannot exceed the pool ceiling (lenient HR 0.977 / strict HR 0.755 — what's in Gecko's top-20). Multilingual requirement (ties to R3): the embedder chosen in the bake-off should be multilingual from the start — real nurse queries may be in Swahili or nonstandard English, and the corpus is planned to grow German-language textbooks. A multilingual embedding space handles cross-lingual query↔chunk matching at the representation level, which is far cheaper on-device than translation pipelines. Gecko's cross-lingual behavior is currently unmeasured.

R3 — Query rewriting / normalization, on-device P2

Motivation: all current retrieval numbers are an optimistic upper bound — the benchmark queries are clean, well-formed English generated from the corpus itself. Real queries will have typos, abbreviations, colloquial phrasing, and mixed/Swahili language; and the corpus will gain German textbooks. Embedding a messy query directly is exactly where a quantized 768-dim embedder degrades most.

On-device-feasible options, in order of increasing cost:

  1. Measure first (eval-side, cheap): build a "messy query" variant set of the benchmark — programmatic typos, medical-abbreviation substitutions, colloquial paraphrases, Swahili translations — and measure Gecko's degradation. This tells us whether rewriting is worth its latency before we build anything. (This is the E1 work item.)
  2. Dictionary normalization: on-device spelling correction + medical-abbreviation expansion (PPH → postpartum haemorrhage). No model call, microseconds, zero risk.
  3. LLM rewrite with the deployed Gemma: the model is already resident — a short "rewrite this as a clear clinical query in English" call before embedding. Costs one extra prefill+decode (must be measured with latency_eval against the TTFT budget); also a natural place to bridge query language → corpus language for the German-textbook scenario.
  4. Conversational query contextualization (same machinery, distinct gap): the deployed pipeline embeds only the latest user turn (mirrored in retrieval_eval/precompute_retrieval.py). A follow-up like "and how much do I give?" carries no retrievable content on its own, so retrieval silently fails on exactly the turns where the nurse is asking for specifics. Options, cheapest first: concatenate the last N turns into the embedded query; or fold follow-up resolution into the Gemma rewrite above. Any fix should be evaluated on the multi-turn HealthBench rows, which are the only multi-turn data we currently have.
  5. Multilingual embedder instead of rewriting (preferred long-term): if R2c lands a multilingual embedder, language bridging happens in embedding space and rewriting reduces to typo/abbreviation normalization only.
Standing note for all of R1–R3 (not an improvement direction — a validation rule): every retrieval change must be validated end-to-end before adoption. Retrieval metrics (HR / Precision / pool recall) are necessary but not sufficient. The Phase B SAQ results show that bundle content changes the generator's behavior, not just its answer content — injected context has triggered scope-refusals (zero-recall rows shifting from 50% to 67% scope-refusal under RAG) — so a retriever that wins HR@3 can still lose key-fact recall. The standard acceptance gate for any change from R1–R3 is: (1) stratified SAQ ±RAG A/B (key-fact recall must not regress, refusal rate must not rise), (2) safety-distribution check (no new potentially_harmful/dangerous mass), (3) on-device latency and memory within budget.

2. Generator-behavior improvements (system prompt) P0

Evidence base: Phase B SAQ + rubric reports (phase-b-saq-result-20260609.html, phase-b-rubric-result-20260609.html).

  • SAQ: 26.7% of kenya responses convey zero key facts; of those, ~50% are scope refusals and ~41% defer-to-doctor without first-line content — i.e. ~91% behavioral, ~8% genuine knowledge misses.
  • Rubric: 61% of failing criteria are deflection; active commission ≈ 2%. The model treats referral as a substitute for management instead of a complement.

Prompt levers (from the SAQ report, all prompt-only):

  1. Reframe "either/or" → "both/and": give concrete first-line management and then advise escalation.
  2. Authorize naming first-line drugs/doses with a "confirm against local protocol" caveat.
  3. Fix geographic over-refusal: the vignette's country must not trigger scope refusal.
  4. Widen the in-scope definition (family planning, GBV care).

Process: draft revised system_en.txt/system_sw.txt language, A/B on a stratified kenya sample (same judge, cluster) before committing to a full Phase B rerun. Watch the safety distribution for regressions — the current prompt's caution is also what keeps commission at 2%; the goal is moving "defer-only" mass to "manage-then-refer", not removing caution.

Faithfulness must be re-checked after any deflection fix. A refusing model makes no claims and cannot hallucinate; today's faithfulness (~9%) and safety (zero "dangerous") numbers are measured on a model that deflects a third of the time. Prompting it to engage with concrete drugs/doses mechanically increases exposure to contradiction and unsupported addition. The G1 acceptance gate is therefore three-sided: SAQ recall up, safety distribution not worse, and a faithfulness spot-check (rerun the oracle-track Lynx pass, or a sample of it, on the new prompt) not materially worse.
Interaction with retrieval: prompt fixes and R1 compound — part of the refusal behavior is context-triggered (injected Zanzibar chunks → geographic refusal). Fixing only the prompt leaves noise-triggered refusals; fixing only the threshold leaves the deflection reflex. Plan to A/B them jointly (2×2 if budget allows).

Faithfulness — no generator-side fix recommended

The oracle-track result (calibrated true-hallucination ≈ 9%, wide band 3–17%, strict rubric) does not motivate generator-side work: most raw Lynx flags are omission/refusal — the same deflection reflex G1 targets — and ~37% of the genuine contradiction flags trace to the corpus contradicting itself, not the model. The improvement work it does motivate lives in the corpus (C1) and eval (E1) sections below: contradiction cleanup, contradiction-aware scoring, and a clinician pass to pin the rate.

3. Corpus / data-quality improvements P2

  • Resolve the 16 confirmed self-contradictory contexts (v0.2.0 audit). File the query IDs and passages upstream against mamai-medical-guidelines / mamaretrieval; 3 conflicts (anaphylaxis adrenaline route, antihypertensive threshold, neonatal head position) are clinically meaningful and need a guideline owner's decision regardless of the eval. Source: oracle-self-contradictions v0.1.0/v0.2.0 reports.
  • Establish a source-precedence policy. The dominant v0.2.0 pattern (8/16) is cross-guideline disagreement — WHO vs NICE vs MSF vs textbooks each retrieved as its own chunk. Individual fixes don't scale; the corpus needs a declared precedence order (or per-topic authoritative source) so conflicting chunks can be resolved or deprioritized systematically.
  • Expect this to grow with retrieval improvements — re-audit after any R2 change. The contradiction count scales with chunk-pool size (6 found on the top-3 oracle, 16 on top-20): a better retriever or deeper k will surface more conflicting sources, not fewer. Pair every retrieval upgrade with a contradiction re-audit.
  • Chunk dedup / near-dup collapsing — duplicate-section merging was a root cause in the v0.1.0 contradiction audit and wastes bundle slots (directly caps P@3).
  • When the German textbooks land: they enter the same pipeline — re-chunk, re-embed, and re-run the contradiction audit cross-lingually (conflicts between German and English guidance will be invisible to monolingual checks and to the generator's reader).

4. Eval-side improvements P1

  • End-to-end gating for every retrieval change (non-negotiable): the SAQ report proves bundle content changes generator behavior, not just answer content. A retriever that wins HR@3 can still lose key-fact recall. Standard gate: stratified SAQ ±RAG A/B + safety distribution check + on-device latency budget.
  • Messy/Swahili query set (see R3.1): quantifies the optimism of the clean-English benchmark and provides the regression set for query rewriting and the multilingual bake-off.
  • Report recall honestly: all pool-recall numbers are relative to the 6-retriever top-20 union pool, with one judging process (graded 0–6) thresholded symmetrically (lenient ≥3 / strict ≥5) on both numerator and denominator. They are reliable for comparing retrievers, not as absolute "fraction of corpus knowledge". Judge label noise is higher at the strict cut (85% vs 95% agreement with Opus 4.7).
  • Contradiction-aware faithfulness scoring: the scorer should distinguish "answer disagrees with context" from "context disagrees with itself" — 37% of the v0.2.0 contradiction bucket were corpus self-conflicts wrongly counted against the model. Until then, every faithfulness number carries a known false-FAIL floor.
  • Clinician-adjudicated calibration (the highest-value single eval upgrade): a medical reviewer re-judging the ~96 faithfulness calibration rows would pin the true-hallucination rate (currently a wide 3–17% band from an LLM-only ladder); a similar ~50-row pass on SAQ would anchor the recall/safety numbers to physician ground truth.
  • Keep eval artifacts versioned per config — any new embedder or corpus version invalidates cached embeddings, thresholds, and the contradiction audit; re-run the affected pieces and stamp manifests as before.

5. Suggested sequence

  1. Now (offline, no GPU): R1 threshold sweep + R2a hybrid simulation + R2b oracle-rerank ceiling — all from existing audit files. Output: chosen threshold, go/no-go on hybrid and rerank.
  2. Next: G1 prompt revision drafted and A/B'd on a stratified kenya sample (jointly with the R1 threshold if possible).
  3. Then: R2c multilingual embedder bake-off on mamaretrieval; winner gets the on-device feasibility pass (quantization, latency, memory).
  4. In parallel: E1 messy/Swahili query set; C1 corpus-contradiction filing upstream.
  5. Later: R2d fine-tune/distill if the bake-off winner still leaves a strict-content gap; R3 LLM query rewrite only if the messy-query measurement shows real degradation that normalization + multilingual embedding don't fix.
