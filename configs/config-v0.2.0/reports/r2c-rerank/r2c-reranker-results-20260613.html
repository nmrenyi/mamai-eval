R2c — reranker comparison, in-domain fine-tuning, device parity, and the end-to-end value gate

2026-06-13 · config-v0.2.0 · branch feat/r2-retriever-upgrade-20260613 · successor to the Phase-0 spike and the P0–P4 plan

A fine-tuned tiny cross-encoder is the answer. Across every deployable reranker scored on the held-out test split, fine-tuning the 23 M MiniLM-L6 on the 159 k in-domain graded pairs (149 s of training) lifts rerank P@3 from 0.627 → 0.756 — past zero-shot mxbai-base (0.714, 8× larger) and within 0.04 of the multi-billion-parameter Qwen3 references (0.79). Its score also becomes far more thresholdable (chunk AUC 0.731 → 0.783, approaching the 0.80 R1 bar). The deployed int8 .tflite + on-device tokenizer were verified to reproduce the offline model exactly. Final deployable choice: fine-tuned MiniLM-L6. But the end-to-end value gate is a caution: re-run with the actual on-device generator (Gemma 4 E4B) and judged by gpt-oss-120b, reranking does not improve key-fact recall on the reliable kenya SAQ sample (gecko, the deployed retriever, is the best arm); the strong offline retrieval gain does not translate end-to-end on this model. (An earlier run reporting a +12% recall lift used the wrong generator, gemma3n-e4b, and is retracted.)

Reading the table:

  • Zero-shot, size barely buys quality. Among off-the-shelf models the deployed MiniLM-L6 (23 M, 0.627) ties the much larger bge/ELECTRA bases (~0.64); only mxbai-base jumps to 0.714. MiniLM-L12 is no better than L6.
  • Medical-domain pretraining did not transfer. MedCPT (PubMed query→article) is the worst reranker here (0.561) — this task (nurse questions → guideline prose) is not its training distribution.
  • The strong references are reachable. The 4–8 B Qwen3 rerankers top out at ~0.79 and are the only models clearly above 0.80 on thresholdability — but they cannot run on-device.
  • Fine-tuning a tiny model closes the gap. See below.

P2.5 — in-domain fine-tuning (the decisive lever)

We fine-tuned the deployed MiniLM-L6 on all 158,993 graded (query, chunk) pairs for the train-split queries (regression on grade/6, 2 epochs, DEV-selected, held-out TEST evaluated — no test query ever seen), then re-scored the same test pool. Training took 149 s on one H200.

Two findings:

  • Fine-tuning is the lever, not size. The fine-tuned 23 M MiniLM-L6 beats zero-shot mxbai-base (0.714) at one-eighth the size and, on the strict cut, exceeds the Qwen3-8B reference (0.380 vs 0.369).
  • R1's threshold is revived. Fine-tuned mxbai-base crosses the 0.80 chunk-AUC bar (0.859 ≥3, 0.857 ≥5) — and tops even the Qwen3-8B reference (0.792). For the first time a reranker's score is thresholdable by R1's own criterion, so the abstention/threshold idea R1 shelved for cosine is worth re-testing on a fine-tuned reranker score.
Deployment choice = fine-tuned MiniLM-L6. It is the best quality-per-byte (24 MB int8, 13 ms/pair, proven on the device) and already beats every zero-shot model. Fine-tuned mxbai-base is the quality-ceiling upgrade (≈8× the size/latency, ~2 s for a 20-chunk bundle) for if/when the threshold-revival or the extra ~0.065 P@3 justifies the footprint — it is not the default. The end-to-end value gate below uses the deployable fine-tuned MiniLM-L6.

P0 — does the deployed reranker reproduce the validated quality?

The in-app reranker is the int8 .tflite driven by a hand-ported Kotlin WordPiece tokenizer. We ran the actual deployed artifact (reranker_minilm_l6_int8.tflite) locally over all 9,820 test pairs, fed by both the HuggingFace tokenizer and a faithful Python port of the Kotlin tokenizer, at the deployed seq-len 256.

The deployed int8 model + on-device tokenizer reproduce the offline fp32 model to within 0.001 P@3 and are rank-identical. The "approximate tokenizer parity" caveat from the spike is closed: conversion (fp32→int8→LiteRT) and the tokenizer port are both faithful, and the deployed reranker is trustworthy. Note: this verifies the zero-shot MiniLM-L6 that is currently on-device; deploying the fine-tuned weights requires re-running the same (proven, architecture-identical) conversion + this parity check on the new checkpoint.

P1 — the end-to-end value gate (does reranking improve answers?)

Everything above is retrieval-quality evidence. The R2 standing rule is to validate end-to-end. Three arms isolate the effect, generated with Gemma 4 E4B (the actual on-device generator — app_config.json llm_model = gemma-4-E4B-it.litertlm) over the same questions:

  • A. Gecko top-3 — the currently deployed retriever (full-delta reference).
  • B. hybrid top-3 — Gecko+BM25 RRF (R2a); isolates fusion (A→B).
  • C. hybrid top-20 → fine-tuned MiniLM-L6 rerank → top-3 — isolates reranking (B→C).

Judged by the pinned gpt-oss-120b on key-fact recall / refusal / safety (SAQ: kenya + afrimedqa_saq), plus MCQ accuracy (afrimedqa, auto-scored — does the −1.8 pp RAG gap close?).

Result (Gemma 4 E4B — the actual on-device generator) — the offline retrieval win does not translate end-to-end. Two fine-tuned rerankers are tested as arm C against the same gecko/hybrid baselines (key-fact recall for SAQ, accuracy for MCQ; n in header):

Reading the corrected result honestly:

  • On the reliable sample (kenya SAQ, n=312), reranking does NOT improve key-fact recall. Gecko — the deployed retriever — is the best arm (0.125); hybrid and both rerankers are slightly lower (~0.111–0.113). The differences are within noise, so the honest read is "no benefit," not "regression."
  • afrimedqa_saq (n=37) hints at benefit (mxbai-ft 0.208 > minilm-ft 0.171 > gecko 0.157) but the sample is far too small to weight.
  • MCQ: the gain is fusion, not reranking. hybrid (0.524) and rerank (0.521) both beat gecko (0.481, +4.3 pp), but reranking adds nothing over the hybrid baseline.
  • One real positive: the MiniLM-L6-ft rerank arm has the lowest harm rate (0.151 vs ~0.19); refusal stays near zero throughout.
Why the offline win didn't carry over. The strong offline reranking gain (P@3 0.51→0.76) was measured on the mamaretrieval query set; this value gate runs on kenya/afrimedqa — a different distribution. So either reranking improves retrieval less on kenya queries specifically, or Gemma 4 E4B already extracts what it needs from gecko's top-3 (a model-ceiling effect). The distinguishing diagnostic — measuring the reranker's P@3 on kenya directly, and how often the relevant chunk is already in gecko's top-3 — is the recommended next step.
Verdict (provisional, pending the mxbai MCQ cell): the reranker is a large, real win on offline retrieval metrics, but its end-to-end answer-quality benefit is not demonstrated on the reliable kenya SAQ sample with the actual device generator (Gemma 4 E4B). This does not condemn reranking — it says the current evidence does not justify shipping it for answer quality alone; the kenya-vs-mamaretrieval distribution gap and the model-ceiling question should be resolved first. (An earlier draft reported a +12% recall lift; it used the wrong generator, gemma3n-e4b, and is retracted.)
