Generator × prompt matrix: Gemma 4 vs Gemma 3n, end-to-end and faithfulness

2026-06-22 · config-v0.2.0 · generators Gemma 4 E4B (Q4_0) & Gemma 3n E4B (Q4_0) · prompts baseline / +G1 / +G1+G2 · English · Track A = end-to-end +RAG (EmbeddingGemma) on kenya SAQ & HealthBench-oss · Track B = oracle faithfulness · judges gpt-oss-120b (Track A), Lynx-70B→gpt-5 (Track B)

Scope. This is the Gemma 4 / Gemma 3n half of the planned 3-generator matrix. The Qwen3.5-397B capability ceiling is generating now and will be appended as a third generator block; every conclusion below is about the two deployable on-device candidates and is complete on its own.
Bottom line.
  • The two generators split on a single axis — helpfulness vs. safety — and the split is large. Gemma 3n is the more helpful model (higher recall 0.30–0.39 vs G4 0.14–0.34; ~2× HealthBench), but the less safe and faithful one (end-to-end dangerous answers 4/11/15 vs G4 0/1/0; oracle hallucination 6.3–6.7% vs 2.6–3.6%). Choosing the generator decides this trade; the prompt only moves you along it.
  • The same prompt has opposite value on the two models. Gemma 4 needs G1 — its baseline deflects on 33% of questions (recall 0.14, HealthBench ≈ 0); G1 cuts deflection to 3% and doubles recall at almost no safety cost (0→1 dangerous). Gemma 3n is harmed by it — it already barely deflects (1.9%), so G1/G2 buy little and push dangerous answers 4→11→15. A prompt is not good or bad in isolation; it is good or bad for a given generator's starting failure mode.
  • RAG did not rescue 3n's safety — these with-RAG dangerous counts (4/11/15) are worse than the earlier no-RAG run (4/7/9). Grounding context doesn't help a generator that doesn't faithfully use it.
  • Two tracks, one story. 3n's end-to-end danger is its oracle-faithfulness deficit made visible: poor grounding → confident un-supported claims → harm. The lever is generator RAG-grounding (G-RAG), not prompt tuning.

The two on-device candidates are not "better vs worse" — they are helpful vs safe. 3n is the more capable model (it answers more, deflects less, scores higher on the open rubric), which is exactly why the device was moved to it. But every safety/faithfulness column favours G4 by roughly 2×, and the danger end (clearly wrong dose, wrong drug, missed red-flag referral) is almost exclusively a 3n phenomenon: G4 produced 0–1 dangerous answers across all three prompts; 3n produced 4, 11, and 15.

3 · Reading 2 — the prompt's value is generator-dependent

The G1 (deflection/scope fix) and G2 (consultation-workflow structure) prompts were written to repair a deflection reflex. Whether that helps depends entirely on whether the generator has that reflex.

Gemma 4 — G1 is a clear win (it has the disease the prompt cures)

  • Baseline G4 deflects on 32.7% of kenya questions — recall 0.139, HealthBench essentially 0. This is the "safe but useless" failure: it rarely says anything wrong because it rarely says anything.
  • G1 collapses deflection 32.7% → 3.2% and doubles recall (0.139 → 0.279); +G2 adds structure for a further lift to 0.338. The safety cost is negligible: dangerous stays 0→1→0, harm creeps 12.8→17.0%. On G4 the deflection fix does exactly what it was designed to do, safely.

Gemma 3n — the prompt is a recall-for-safety trade that fails the gate

  • 3n already barely deflects at baseline (1.9%) — the G4→3n upgrade had already solved deflection. So G1/G2 have little deflection to repair; they mostly just push the model to assert more.
  • Recall does rise (0.297 → 0.351 → 0.389), but dangerous answers rise with it: 4 → 11 → 15, and harm jumps to ~34%. The recovered "helpfulness" is partly confident, un-grounded, occasionally dangerous content. On 3n the same prompts buy recall by spending safety — and fail the safety gate.
The matrix resolves an apparent contradiction in the branch history. The G1 prompt-A/B on 3n looked like a failure (harm up, dangerous up); yet G1 is a sound idea. Both are true: G1 is right for a deflecting generator (G4) and wrong for a non-deflecting one (3n). The deployed generator changed the prompt's sign.

4 · Reading 3 — RAG didn't rescue 3n, and the two tracks agree

RAG is not the fix for 3n's safety. These Track-A numbers are with EmbeddingGemma retrieval, and 3n's dangerous count under the prompts (4/11/15) is actually higher than the earlier no-RAG A/B (4/7/9). Handing a poor grounder more context, plus a prompt that suppresses its one safe instinct (deflection), produces more confident wrong answers, not fewer.

The end-to-end danger and the oracle faithfulness deficit are the same fact. 3n hallucinates against gold context 2× more than G4 (categorized 6.3–6.7% vs 2.6–3.6%); under live RAG that ungrounded-assertion tendency surfaces as 2× the harm rate and the cluster of dangerous answers. G4's faithfulness is why it stays safe even when G1 makes it talk more. So the binding lever is the generator's grounding, addressable by generator-side RAG-grounding (G-RAG) — not by retrieval swaps or prompt edits, both of which leave the grounding deficit intact.

6 · Implication for the device decision

  • If staying on Gemma 3n (the current device model, chosen for capability): do not ship G1/G2 as-is. They raise recall but fail the safety gate (dangerous 4→15). 3n already doesn't deflect, so the prompt's intended benefit is mostly already banked; what remains is downside. The real work is grounding 3n's generation (G-RAG), then re-testing safety.
  • Gemma 4 + G1 is the safe-but-modest alternative: near-zero dangerous, deflection fixed, recall doubled — but its capability ceiling (recall ≤0.34, HealthBench ≤0.05) sits below 3n's. It is the choice if safety dominates and the helpfulness bar is low.
  • The frontier comparison still pending (Qwen-397B ceiling) will show how much of 3n's helpfulness/faithfulness gap is model-size-bound vs addressable on-device.

7 · Method

  • Track A (end-to-end, +RAG). Each cell generated with EmbeddingGemma top-3 retrieval, then judged by gpt-oss-120b: kenya SAQ via the behavior judge (key_fact_recall + 4-level safety + 4-level behavior/deflection); HealthBench-oss via the rubric judge (weighted_met = Σ points·met / Σ max(points,0)). Aggregated from the judged run-dirs with the pipeline's own _agg_dataset.
  • Track B (faithfulness). Oracle context (mamaretrieval v0.2.0, score≥5, top-3, n=2,989) → Lynx-70B PASS/FAIL → gpt-5 categorize (genuine hallucination = contradiction + unsupported addition) + calibrate (50-PASS-sample miss-rate extrapolation, wide CIs). Detail in the companion faithfulness report.
  • Both generators Q4_0 GGUF (on-device quantization), config-pinned decoding; identical prompts, retrieval, oracle, and judges across cells — every difference is the generator or the prompt.
  • Provenance. Track A + G4 Track B generated 2026-06-22 (gen-prompt-matrix-20260622); 3n Track B from g1-ab-3n-20260619, identical pipeline.

8 · Caveats

  • English only; Swahili untested.
  • Single judge family per semantic step (gpt-oss-120b for Track A, Lynx→gpt-5 for Track B); the physician-ground-truth judge validation still applies before these rates are treated as absolute.
  • Calibrated faithfulness is a noisy 50-sample extrapolation (see †); categorized is the metric of record.
  • Dangerous counts are small integers on n=312 — directionally clear (0–1 vs 4–15) but individual counts carry sampling noise; the recommended per-case re-adjudication will firm them up.
