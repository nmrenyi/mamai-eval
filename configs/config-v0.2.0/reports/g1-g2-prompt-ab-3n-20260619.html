G1/G2 Prompt A/B on Gemma 3n E4B — does the deflection-fix prompt help?

2026-06-19 · config-v0.2.0 prompts · generator Gemma 3n E4B (Q4_0) · no-RAG · branch feat/g1-prompt-fix-20260611 · experiment g1-ab-3n-20260619

Bottom line — do not ship G1/G2 as-is on Gemma 3n. The prompt levers work mechanically (kenya key-fact recall +40% relative, 0.288 → 0.403; deflection eliminated; HealthBench completeness turns positive) — but on the deployment decision set they buy that helpfulness with a monotonic safety regression: kenya harm-rate 22% → 34% → 35% and dangerous answers 4 → 7 → 9. That fails this repo's mandatory safety gate. And the premise has shifted: the Gemma 4 → 3n upgrade already drove baseline deflection to ~2%, so G1's original job (fix deflection) is largely done — what the prompt now delivers is a recall-for-safety trade, not a deflection repair.

1. Design

Three-arm A/B isolating the effect of the G1 (deflection-fix) and G2-lever-1 (structure) prompt revisions from the improvement plan (docs/g1-prompt-fix-plan.md). Only the open-ended system prompt varies; generator, params, datasets, and judge are held constant.

  • Generator: Gemma 3n E4B (the now-deployed model, app v0.3.0), GGUF Q4_0 cluster proxy.
  • No RAG — isolates the prompt from retrieval-triggered behavior.
  • Datasets: kenya 312 (decision set) + afrimedqa_saq 37 + whb 20 (SAQ) + healthbench_oss_eval 1209 (rubric). All three arms generated 1,578 rows each.
  • Judge: pinned gpt-oss-120b @ medium. SAQ via rescore_open_v2 (4-value behavior tag + key-fact recall + safety enum); rubric via rescore_rubric (weighted_met / positive / penalty / 5 axes). 0 judge failures across all SAQ rows.

2. Headline — the kenya SAQ tradeoff

kenya is the physician-authored clinical decision set (n=312) and the experiment's primary readout. Recall and harm rise together and monotonically with each prompt lever:

Read it as a dose–response curve. Every lever that adds engagement/completeness (recall 0.288 → 0.365 → 0.403) also adds commission (harm 22% → 34% → 35%, dangerous 4 → 7 → 9). The most helpful arm (arm3) is the least safe. Recall gain is +7.7 pp (G1) then +3.8 pp (structure), cumulative +11.5 pp (+40% relative); harm gain is +11.6 pp then +1.3 pp, cumulative +13.1 pp; dangerous answers more than double.

Robustness

  • The individual recall steps (+7.7, +3.8 pp) sit in the plan's "<10 pp may be judge noise" band — but they are monotonic across three arms, a consistent signal rather than noise.
  • The harm signal is above the noise floor: arm1→arm3 harm +13.1 pp and dangerous 4→9 (a doubling) — the clearest, most robust effect in the dataset, and it points the wrong way.
  • Baseline deflection is already 1.9% (6 of 312 rows). The Gemma 4 → 3n swap removed the deflection problem the plan was written to fix; G1/G2 only close the last ~2%.

4. HealthBench rubric — a different, milder story

On the rubric track G1/G2 look mildly positive: weighted_met and the completeness axis rise, and the penalty rate is flat — no safety regression visible here.

All three arms scored at full n=1209. weighted_met rises with G1 then plateaus (arm2 ≈ arm3); the completeness axis rises monotonically (−0.058 → +0.026 → +0.044); penalty stays flat (~0.36).

Why the two tracks diverge. They are different question sets, not the same answers scored twice. kenya is free-form clinical decision support, where the SAQ safety enum directly flags wrong/dangerous management. HealthBench's penalty is criterion-based on a different (largely information-seeking) question distribution and does not catch the same commission risk. For a safety gate on the deployed product, the kenya SAQ signal governs — and it is adverse.

5. Verdict

The three-sided acceptance gate requires all of: recall up, safety not worse, faithfulness not worse. Helpfulness passes; safety fails on the decision set. Net recommendation:

  • Do not ship G1 or G1+G2 as-is on Gemma 3n.
  • Re-scope the work. 3n already eliminated deflection, so the deflection-fix framing is obsolete. The remaining value is completeness — pursue it through the levers that do not drive commission (structure/skeleton, addressing-the-clinician, local-context) and drop or heavily guard the dose-authorization + forced-engagement levers that the harm curve implicates.
  • The real safety path is grounding, not prompting. The commission risk is the model asserting concrete drugs/doses from parametric memory. That argues for pairing engagement with RAG and measuring RAG-context faithfulness (the oracle-track Lynx pass) — the natural next experiment.

6. Caveats

  • Single run. No bootstrap CIs yet; recall steps are near the per-run noise floor (the harm/dangerous trend is above it). A repeat judge pass on the safety axis would harden the gate decision.
  • Faithfulness proxy. These arms are no-RAG, so the plan's oracle-track Lynx faithfulness pass (faithfulness to retrieved context) is not directly applicable; the SAQ safety enum (potentially_harmful + dangerous) is used as the commission proxy. A true RAG faithfulness rerun is the follow-up if engagement is pursued with retrieval.
  • Q4_0 cluster proxy for the on-device .litertlm int4 — direction is robust, absolute magnitudes will shift on-device.
  • Absolute harm is high (22% baseline) partly because 3n is a less-safe base model (flagged in the R2c synthesis) and potentially_harmful is a broad category; the governing evidence is the delta, not the absolute level.
  • All tracks scored at full n (SAQ 312/37/20; rubric 1209 × 3 arms); 0 judge failures.
