MAM-AI — model-size × precision × RAG ladder

2026-06-24. Generator ladder from on-device 2–4B to a 397B frontier MoE, all at the deployed G1 prompt, evaluated on kenya (312 nurse-written primary-care vignettes; recall = key-fact recall, harm = harm rate, defl = deflection) and HealthBench-oss (1,209 items; weighted_met nets completeness (pos) against penalties (pen)). Judge: pinned gpt-oss-120b (kenya via rescore_open_v2, HealthBench via rescore_rubric) — same pipeline as the matrix. Gemma rungs are GGUF via llama.cpp (on-device-faithful); Qwen rungs are vLLM FP8/bf16 (server reference).

Research questions: (1) does a bigger model carry better medical knowledge (no-RAG)? (2) how much does precision matter at the deployed size? (3) does RAG still help as the model scales? (4) does the offline Gecko→EmbeddingGemma retrieval win convert to online answer gains?

Takeaways

  • Bigger → better, but it peaks at ~30B and then plateaus/dips. HealthBench weighted_met climbs from ~0.05–0.10 (2–4B) to a peak ~0.26–0.27 at 27–35B, then declines at 122B (0.19) and 397B (0.23). Kenya recall likewise peaks at 27–35B (~0.47) and dips at 122–397B (0.42–0.44). Caveat: 122/397B are Qwen 3.5 while 27/35B are Qwen 3.6 — a generation boundary confounds the very top — but the practical message holds: a ~30B model is the sweet spot; the 397B frontier is not better for this task.
  • Safety improves monotonically with size. Kenya harm rate falls 0.26 (E2B) → 0.16 (E4B) → 0.06–0.09 (26–31B) and deflection collapses to ~0. Bigger models are safer even where the recall plateaus.
  • Precision barely matters at the deployed size. E4B Q4→Q8→BF16 is flat on kenya (0.33/0.33/0.32) and only +0.017 on HealthBench (0.054→0.071). int4 deployment loses almost nothing — a hospital server gains far more from a bigger model than from de-quantizing the 4B.
  • RAG is net-negative, and increasingly so as the model grows. Every Gemma rung scores lower with EmbeddingGemma RAG than no-RAG, and the gap widens with size (E4B −0.02, 31B −0.10 on HealthBench; kenya recall drops at every size). The generator's parametric knowledge already exceeds what retrieved context adds, and the context distracts the more capable models more.
  • The offline Gecko→EmbeddingGemma retrieval win does NOT convert to answers. Despite EG's +12–13pp offline P@3, at the deployed E4B-int4+G1 the online gain over Gecko is tiny (kenya recall +0.011, HealthBench +0.009), and both retrievers underperform no-RAG (recall 0.279/0.268 vs 0.328; HB 0.038/0.029 vs 0.054). Retrieval quality is not the binding constraint — the generator's ability to use context is.
  • Bottom line for MAM-AI: the lever is generator capability, not retrieval or precision. The deployed 4B-int4 is a safe floor; a ~30B server-class model is the single biggest upgrade (HealthBench ~5×, kenya recall +0.14, harm halved) and you'd stop well before 397B. On-device, neither a better embedder nor higher precision moves open-ended quality much.
