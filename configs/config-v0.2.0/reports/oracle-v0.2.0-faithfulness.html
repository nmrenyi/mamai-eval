Generator Faithfulness — Gemma 4 E4B

Does Gemma 4 E4B use retrieved clinical-guideline context faithfully — without contradicting it or making claims it doesn't support? Tested in isolation under oracle context, so any failure is attributable to the generator, not retrieval.

3. FAIL categorization (n = 174, gpt-5 medium reasoning)

Lynx's FAIL bucket conflates several distinct things. The §3.1 spec defines a faithfulness failure (a "real hallucination") narrowly — only contradiction or unsupported addition. Cases Lynx flags for other reasons (the answer was incomplete, declined, or unclear) are not faithfulness failures under the spec, even though Lynx marks them FAIL.

CategoryWhat it meansReal hallucination?
contradiction Answer asserts something that conflicts with the context — wrong dose, wrong threshold, states X where the context says Y. yes
unsupported_addition Answer makes a clinical claim that isn't supported by the context — information introduced beyond the document. yes
omission Answer is correct as far as it goes but leaves out content the context contains. Lynx penalises incompleteness; §3.1 does not. no
refusal Answer declines to engage ("consult a doctor", "out of scope") even when the context could substantively answer. no
unclear Lynx's reasoning doesn't let you identify the failure mode confidently. —

The point of this section is not precise per-category precision (n=10 per bucket is too small for that) — it's an audit of whether gpt-5 actually applies the strict §3.1 rubric correctly, especially on the rules that broke gpt-oss-120b.

Net adherence across all 16 gpt-5-FAIL verdicts in the calibration sample: 16/16 are rubric-defensible. The earlier gpt-oss-120b attempt drifted heavily on exactly the cases the rubric prescribes PASS (called FAIL on refusals, omissions, clarification questions) — gpt-5 does not. This is the direct evidence that the v0.2.0 calibrated rate isn't contaminated by the same judge-side rubric drift that disqualified gpt-oss.

Sample sizes (10 per FAIL-bucket, 6 for unclear, 50 for the PASS stratum) are too small for precise per-category precision estimates but large enough to detect systematic rubric drift — which is what this audit is testing.

Caveat — gpt-5 examining gpt-5. The 12 Lynx-FAIL bucket labels in the chart are from gpt-5's own categorize step on v0.2.0. The 4 Lynx-PASS rubric categories in the mini-table were assigned manually by a Claude reader of gpt-5's verdict reasoning. So this whole section is closer to a self-consistency check on gpt-5 than an independent audit — a clinician or different-family LLM would be required for that. The signal is illustrative (gpt-5's verdicts are internally consistent with the rubric it was given), not load-bearing on the headline rate.

4.4 Population estimate

Extrapolate from the calibration sample to all 2,989 responses by treating each Lynx stratum's confirmed-FAIL fraction as an estimator for that stratum's whole population:

Note the missed-FAIL term (225) dominates the FAIL-stratum term (45). Lynx misses real hallucinations 4× more often than it correctly flags them — a small per-row miss rate becomes a big number when applied to 2,815 rows.

5. Methodology choices

  • Judge = gpt-5 (OpenAI Batch API, Structured Outputs with json_schema, strict=true). Categorize at reasoning_effort=medium; calibrate at high. 3 length-truncated calibrate rows re-run sync at max_completion_tokens=16384.
  • Family independence: gpt-5 is different family from Lynx (Llama-3), the oracle's relevance labeler (Qwen 3.5), and the generator (Gemma) — no shared blind spots with the model being audited.
  • gpt-oss-120b was tried first and rejected: failed all three pre-committed v0.1.0 validation gates (rubric drift on refusal/omission cases). Evidence preserved in the v0.1.0 run dir.
  • Pinned rubrics: CATEGORIZE_RUBRIC and CALIBRATION_RUBRIC are module-level constants in analyze_lynx_fails.py and calibrate.py — same definitions used on v0.1.0, single source of truth.
  • Cost: $1.78 total (categorize batch $0.90, calibrate batch $0.74, sync retries $0.14). Under the $5 budget.

7. Limitations

  • LLM judge, not human ground truth. gpt-5 is a careful rubric-follower but isn't a clinician. A medical reviewer pass on the 96 calibration rows would pin the rate where it now brackets.
  • Single judge. No triangulation — we are committed to gpt-5's interpretation of the strict rubric.
  • CI on miss rate is wide ([2%, 16%]) — based on 4 / 50 events. The population estimate inherits that uncertainty.
  • Stability not tested. Run-to-run variance, paraphrase sensitivity, greedy-vs-sampled — all §3.2 work, not in this run.
  • v0.1.0 baseline is soft. See §6 — the 0.33% is a subagent estimate, not a benchmark.

8. Next steps

  1. Self-contradiction re-audit on v0.2.0 contradiction-bucket FAILs (43 cases) — surface false-FAILs caused by the oracle context itself contradicting itself, as the v0.1.0 audit did (6 cases there). Substring-matching task, no LLM judge needed.
  2. Human-adjudicated calibration (optional, the gold standard the spec ultimately wants) — clinician re-judges ~50–100 rows, pins the rate.
