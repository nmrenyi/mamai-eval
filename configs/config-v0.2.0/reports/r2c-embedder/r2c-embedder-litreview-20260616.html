R2c — On-Device Embedder Literature Review

2026-06-16 · config-v0.2.0 · P0.1 of r2c-embedder-bakeoff-next-steps · Goal: shortlist 2–3 deployable embedder candidates to beat Gecko, and feed the G2 feasibility spike + the harness prefix fix. License excluded as a filter (pure research). Multilingual deprioritized to a tiebreaker.

Three findings that revise the plan's premises — read before the tables:
  1. The deployed Gecko is English-only, not multilingual. The shipped artifact is litert-community/Gecko-110m-en (~110M, 768-dim, int8 TFLite ~112–146 MB). The plan's "Gecko is multilingual" premise was wrong — so adding multilingual is a differentiator, not a parity requirement, and the multilingual hard-filter was guarding a property the baseline never had.
  2. The feasibility intuition is inverted. EmbeddingGemma is the lowest-risk on-device option — Google already ships an official LiteRT export with on-device NPU benchmarks. Classic BERT/XLM-R sentence-transformers are higher risk on LiteRT specifically (no official TFLite; the community runs them on ONNX Runtime). The plan's "BERT-family is the safe path" assumption holds for ONNX, not LiteRT.
  3. The runtime choice (LiteRT vs ONNX Runtime Mobile) gates the whole candidate set — and it's an app-team decision, not a research one. See §7.

7. Recommended shortlist & the runtime fork

Take these into G2 (feasibility spike) → offline screen:
  1. EmbeddingGemma-300M — primary. The only candidate whose LiteRT gate is already cleared by Google; multilingual (a genuine upgrade over English-only Gecko); MRL (768→256) flexes footprint and vector-store size; has a working medical fine-tune precedent. De-risk early: it ships mixed int4/int8 (not pure int8) via QAT, has no MediaPipe .task (wire SentencePiece + prefixes yourself), and has two open conversion bugs that silently produce garbage vectors — validate exported embeddings vs PyTorch (allclose) before trusting any metric.
  2. MedCPT (query encoder on-device + offline article index) — the strongest in-domain evidence (only model here with real BioASQ/BEIR-med retrieval numbers). The asymmetric design neutralizes its dual-encoder storage cost. Validate: it was trained on PubMed query–article pairs — confirm transfer to guideline prose; 64-token query cap; English-only.
  3. MedEmbed-small (bge-small base) — best footprint (~32 MB int8) with a verified LiteRT conversion, and the cheapest base to medically fine-tune later (R2d). Validate: int8 quality on a 33M model is the most at-risk regime — test ranking after quantization.

Hold in reserve: multilingual-e5-small/base if multilingual is re-prioritized and the runtime question (below) resolves toward ONNX or a successful LiteRT spike.

Decision that gates everything — confirm with the app team before the spike: is the on-device runtime strictly LiteRT, or is ONNX Runtime Mobile acceptable?
  • If strictly LiteRT → EmbeddingGemma is the de-risked path; classic encoders (e5/bge/MedEmbed/MedCPT) are convertible but carry real validation effort, and the custom-arch models (gte/arctic) are high-risk.
  • If ONNX Runtime Mobile is allowed → the entire e5 / bge / MedEmbed / MedCPT family becomes low-risk (it's the proven community path), widening the shortlist considerably and making the multilingual e5 models cheap to try.

Open risks to close empirically (none resolvable from the literature)

  • int8 LiteRT embedding quality is under-documented — no public TFLite-int8 retrieval-quality numbers exist except Google's own EmbeddingGemma (which doesn't publish a retention delta). Small models (33M) degrade most. Must measure ranking post-quantization (fall back to int16-activation or fp16 if cosine geometry breaks).
  • MedCPT domain transfer — PubMed literature ≠ clinical guideline prose.
  • No biomedical embedder has a LiteRT precedent — the mechanics are standard BERT, but the domain-on-LiteRT combination is new.
  • Cross-benchmark numbers are not comparable — the only clean gap comes from running candidates + one ceiling through the frozen in-house split.
  • Vector-store sizing — at 768-dim fp32 the store is ~190 MB; a 256-dim MRL build or int8 embeddings cuts it 4–8× and may matter more than model size on the device budget.

Method & sources

Five parallel research passes (EmbeddingGemma deep-dive; multilingual small encoders; English + medical-domain encoders; heavy models + reference ceilings; LiteRT/AI-Edge conversion feasibility), each source-backed against primary sources (HF model cards, arXiv tech reports, Google AI Edge / LiteRT docs, MTEB/MMTEB/MIRACL/MedTEB leaderboards) with an adversarial verification pass. Quantitative claims are cited in the underlying fact sheets; figures marked "vendor" are self-reported and not independently benchmarked. Baseline Gecko facts confirmed against this repo's latency_eval, retrieval_eval/retrieval.py, and the v0.2 improvement-plan report.
