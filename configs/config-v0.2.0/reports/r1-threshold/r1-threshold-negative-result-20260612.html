R1 — Retrieval relevance threshold: negative result

2026-06-12 · config-v0.2.0 · branch feat/r1-retrieval-threshold-20260611 · closes the P0 item “R1 — Relevance threshold tuning” of the v0.2 improvement plan

Gecko’s cosine similarity score carries no usable information about whether a retrieved chunk — or a whole bundle — is relevant. No threshold rule on it (absolute, relative-margin, or gated) can suppress the harmful RAG injections without abstaining on a comparable share of the helpful ones. No threshold ships: retrieval.similarity_threshold stays 0.0, and the planned sweep/election/ratification stages (Figures 3–5, Tiers 2–3) are cancelled as moot.

1. Question and pre-registered decision rule

RAG currently harms the system: −1.8 pp on MCQ accuracy, and SAQ zero-recall rows shift toward scope-refusal when off-topic chunks are injected. R1 proposed the cheapest fix: calibrate a per-chunk relevance cutoff on Gecko’s cosine scores so junk bundles trigger fall-back to no-RAG (“abstention over noise”).

Per docs/r1-threshold-tuning-plan.md, two fail-fast gates were fixed before looking at the data:

  • Figure 1 (label gate): can the score separate relevant (judge grade ≥ 3) from junk chunks? AUC ≳ 0.80 → absolute cutoff viable; 0.65–0.80 → relative rules; ≤ 0.60 → stop, file a negative result.
  • Figure 2 (outcome gate): do the bundles that actually flipped end-to-end outcomes for the worse sit at visibly lower scores than those that helped? If not, filtering cannot close the −1.8 pp gap.

Both gates failed, on three independent test surfaces.

All four statistics are computed from Table A’s top-3 rows by retrieval_eval/plot_threshold_signal.py (values in threshold_signal_metrics.json). The chunk-level AUCs pool all 9,555 chunks, so most compared pairs come from different queries — they test whether score magnitudes are comparable corpus-wide, the assumption behind an absolute cutoff. The within-query concordance restricts the comparison to pairs from the same bundle: across the 4,182 (relevant, junk) pairs that co-occur in a top-3 bundle, the relevant chunk has the higher cosine 62.3% of the time — the signal available to relative rules, which only compare a bundle against itself. The bundle-level AUC scores each of the 3,185 bundles by its top-1 (or mean) cosine and asks whether that predicts containing any relevant chunk (base rate 81.4%) — the signal available to gated abstention.

The practical reading is in the PR panel: today’s unfiltered bundle precision is 0.477 and the R1 target was ~0.7–0.8. Even at τ = 0.80 — which discards 75% of the genuinely relevant chunks and keeps only 21% of all chunks — precision reaches just 0.563. The target region is unreachable at any threshold.

Reconciling this with Gecko’s 81% hit rate

These results do not say Gecko is useless — per query, it does real work. HR@3 = 0.814 means four of five bundles contain at least one relevant chunk. A random baseline makes the contrast concrete. The true number of relevant chunks per query is unknown: the judged pool (union of six retrievers’ top-20) finds ~20 per query, but that is a lower bound, and the pool is demonstrably unsaturated — 41.5% of relevant chunks were surfaced by only one of the six retrievers, so more presumably exist outside the pool. The comparison therefore inverts the question instead of estimating: for random top-3 draws from the 63,650-chunk corpus to match Gecko’s 81% hit rate, a query would need 1 − (1 − R/63,650)³ = 0.814, i.e. R ≈ 27,000 relevant chunks — 43% of the entire corpus relevant to a single clinical question. Even a modest 10% hit rate would require ~2,200 relevant chunks per query (3.5% of the corpus). Whatever the true count is, it is nowhere near that, so Gecko’s lift over random is orders of magnitude: it reliably finds chunks on the right topic.

The threshold, however, needed a different ability: among the on-topic chunks that already made the top-3, tell the ones that actually answer the question (47.7%) from the ones that merely share its vocabulary. There the score fails twice over. Across queries, score magnitudes are not comparable — one query’s 0.75 is no more trustworthy than another’s 0.68 — so no fixed cutoff works (AUC 0.572). And within a single bundle, the ordering is nearly random with respect to true relevance — the genuinely useful chunk outscores the junk one only 62% of the time — so margin-from-top-1 rules fail too. Gecko can find the topic but cannot grade usefulness within it, and a threshold can only act on the second ability: by the time scores are compared, the topic-finding work is already done.

5. Secondary finding — platform-dependent scores

The same quantized Gecko TFLite model produces different cosine scores per platform: comparing the cluster (x86) audit rankings against a local Apple-silicon run of the identical script and assets over 1,793 shared pairs — median |Δ| 0.004, p90 0.012, max 0.063; top-3 membership agrees only 84.3% at chunk level. The deployment target (Android/ARM) is a third platform. Consequence: any absolute score threshold would carry a ~±0.02 platform noise band on top of its (absent) signal, and future score-based logic must be calibrated on the deployment platform, not the cluster.

6. Is thresholding dead in general, or just for Gecko?

The Stage-1 gate costs nothing to run for the other five audit retrievers — their top-20 rankings are in rankings.parquet against the same judgments. medcpt and octen published rank-only data, so the two pooled score statistics are not computable for them; the within-bundle concordance survives, because inside a bundle rank order is score order. Stage 2 is not computable for any of them, as no end-to-end runs used their retrievals.

Three readings:

  • No retriever passes the bar. Even voyage — the API-only quality ceiling — sits at 0.73–0.76 on all three score statistics, below the 0.80 viability bar, and octen (the strongest cluster-feasible ranker, concordance 0.762) is the only value that touches it. Score-based filtering is at best marginal even with the best retrievers tested, and hopeless on Gecko’s scores. Thresholding should not be assumed to revive automatically under R2.
  • Score quality is a separate axis from ranking quality. lateon ranks well (P@3 = 0.738, HR@3 = 0.971) yet its pooled chunk AUC is 0.501 — its score magnitudes carry zero cross-query meaning, while its within-bundle ordering (0.708) is strong. medcpt is the inverse cautionary case: weak ranking (P@3 = 0.335) and the weakest concordance (0.603) of all six. A bake-off that only checked P@k would see none of this — which is exactly why both gate statistics belong in the R2 screen.
  • BM25’s scores beat Gecko’s on every computable statistic — notably bundle-level junk detection (0.684 vs 0.574) — while being deployable on-device. This strengthens the R2a hybrid case: lexical scores add not just complementary recall but a more meaningful confidence signal than the embedding provides.

7. Decision

  • No threshold ships. params.json → retrieval.similarity_threshold remains 0.0; a nonzero value would be a placebo with platform-noise side effects.
  • Figures 3–5 and Tiers 2–3 of the plan are cancelled. Frontier curves over a near-chance classifier and an H200 election among indistinguishable candidates would spend compute to no end.
  • The M1 “gap collapses” acceptance rerun is moot for R1 (nothing changed to validate); it remains the standing probe for R2/C1 changes.

8. Implications for the improvement plan

  • R2 (retriever upgrade) inherits R1’s priority. The harm mechanism is not “low-scoring junk we failed to filter” — Gecko retrieves junk confidently. Score quality must become an explicit R2 bake-off criterion: every candidate embedder gets a Figure-1 AUC on these same committed tables before any device work. The table builders in retrieval_eval/ are the harness for exactly that.
  • R2 candidates must be score-checked on the deployment platform (see §5) if any score-based logic is ever revisited.
  • G1 (prompt-side robustness to junk context) is now the only near-term lever for the RAG-hurts problem, since the retrieval-side cheap fix is ruled out. The G1/R1 2×2 A/B planned for SAQ reduces to a prompt-only A/B.
  • SAQ stays untouched. The 369 open-ended rows were never consumed by this analysis and remain a clean held-out set for the next cycle.
